Boston Airbnb Revenue Optimization

Applied analytics project examining how pricing, minimum-night requirements, listing size, host quality, and neighborhood affect Airbnb revenue performance in Boston.

Project Overview

This project analyzed Boston Airbnb listing-level data to understand what factors influence revenue and occupancy performance. The analysis focused on pricing strategy, minimum-night requirements, listing size, host reputation, and neighborhood effects.

The goal was to identify patterns that could help short-term rental hosts make better decisions about pricing, booking flexibility, property positioning, and location strategy.

Business Problem

Airbnb hosts operate in a market where guests rely on visible listing characteristics to judge value before booking. Because hosts cannot directly control every aspect of demand, the project examined which listing attributes and host-controlled decisions were most strongly associated with revenue outcomes.

The central questions were whether pricing strategies and minimum-night requirements affect occupancy and revenue, and whether neighborhood location meaningfully changes a listing’s revenue-generating ability.

Methods and Workflow

  • Cleaned and prepared Airbnb listing data using Excel and Python.
  • Removed unnecessary columns, handled missing values, and converted relevant fields into usable data types.
  • Explored listing performance across price, minimum-night requirements, neighborhood, bedrooms, beds, accommodations, and host quality indicators.
  • Created visualizations to compare revenue and occupancy patterns across listing segments.
  • Built regression models using control variables, main effects, and interaction terms to evaluate revenue drivers.
  • Translated statistical results into business recommendations for hosts and property decision-makers.

Key Findings

  • Listings with more flexible minimum-night requirements generally performed better than listings with stricter stay requirements.
  • Downtown Boston showed stronger revenue performance than many other neighborhoods, suggesting that location was an important demand driver.
  • Medium-to-large listings could generate strong revenue, but larger listings also carried higher vacancy risk depending on location and demand.
  • Host quality indicators, such as Superhost status, response rate, and review count, were meaningful signals associated with revenue performance.
  • Regression results suggested that minimum-night requirements, neighborhood, listing size, and host reputation should be considered together rather than in isolation.

Business Recommendations

  • Prioritize flexible booking policies to avoid reducing the pool of potential guests.
  • Use neighborhood demand patterns when evaluating pricing and property investment decisions.
  • Emphasize host reliability signals, such as response rate, guest reviews, and Superhost status.
  • Consider listing size and location together, since larger properties may perform best in higher-demand areas.
  • Use both revenue and occupancy when evaluating performance, rather than relying on one metric alone.
