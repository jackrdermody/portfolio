Gildan Demand Forecasting

Hierarchical forecasting project using retail POS data, machine learning, and demand drivers to support SKU-level production, capacity, and materials planning decisions.

Project Overview

This project focused on forecasting weekly retail sales for a clothing manufacturer across multiple product styles and sizes. The business challenge was to create SKU-level forecasts that could support operational planning while remaining consistent across higher-level product group and total portfolio forecasts.

The final goal was to forecast January 2025 through December 2025 demand and evaluate which forecasting approach best supported supply chain decisions such as production, capacity planning, and materials purchasing.

Business Problem

Different teams require forecasts at different levels of detail. Supply planning needs accurate top-line forecasts for materials and capacity decisions, while operations teams need more granular size-level SKU forecasts for cutting, sewing, and inventory planning.

The project evaluated how to generate forecasts that were accurate at the SKU level while also reconciling cleanly to broader product group and total portfolio views.

Methods and Workflow

  • Prepared weekly POS, pricing, shipment, inventory, and distribution data for forecasting.
  • Created time-series features including lag variables, rolling averages, calendar features, and seasonal indicators.
  • Compared baseline forecasting methods against machine learning and sequence-based approaches.
  • Tested models including naive forecasting, ARIMA, Random Forest, XGBoost, and GRU.
  • Used time-series cross-validation and hyperparameter tuning to improve model performance.
  • Evaluated forecasts across SKU, product group, and total portfolio levels.
  • Compared bottom-up and top-down hierarchical reconciliation approaches.

Modeling Approach

The project began with simple baseline methods to establish a reference point, then moved into more advanced models capable of capturing nonlinear relationships and demand drivers. XGBoost was selected as the strongest model because it provided high forecast accuracy while still allowing for interpretation through feature importance and SHAP-style analysis.

Key drivers included prior-week demand, seasonal timing, recent rolling demand averages, price, retailer inventory, and distribution-related signals. The project also evaluated the tradeoff between forecast accuracy and model explainability.

Key Results

  • XGBoost outperformed baseline, Random Forest, and GRU models across the project’s forecast evaluation levels.
  • Bottom-up reconciliation preserved SKU-level demand behavior better than top-down allocation.
  • Forecast accuracy improved at higher aggregation levels, while lower-volume SKUs remained more difficult to predict.
  • Price, retailer inventory, lag demand, and seasonal effects were important demand signals.
  • The final recommendation was to use different forecast signals depending on the planning horizon.

Business Recommendations

  • Use short-term SKU-level forecasts for production and inventory allocation decisions.
  • Use quarterly product group forecasts for capacity and labor planning.
  • Use longer-horizon aggregated forecasts for materials planning, with forecast ranges rather than point estimates.
  • Adjust production planning for under-forecast bias to reduce stockout risk and improve service levels.
  • Incorporate additional external data such as promotional calendars, retailer POS feeds, distribution velocity, and macroeconomic indicators to improve future forecast quality.
